## Materials & Methods We Use for Cabinet Refinishing

- **Catalyzed conversion varnish** — cures to a hard, moisture-resistant shell that holds up better than standard lacquer in the Midlands' humid summers
- **Sprayed alkyd enamel** — lays down a smooth, brush-mark-free finish for painted cabinet doors and drawer fronts
- **Wood grain filler** — evens out open-grain oak surfaces before painting so the final coat looks smooth, not textured
- **Moisture-resistant primer** — seals cabinet boxes and doors against the swelling that humid weather can cause over time
- **Soft-close hinge hardware** — replaces worn hinges with quieter, longer-lasting mechanisms during refacing or hardware upgrades
- **Refacing veneer** — matches new doors to real-wood veneer over existing boxes for a full-replacement look at a lower cost

## Permits, Codes & Compliance for Cabinet Refinishing

### Usually needs a permit
- Full kitchen remodels that relocate plumbing or electrical
- Structural changes to walls for built-in cabinetry
- Additions to the kitchen's footprint

### Usually no permit
- Cabinet refinishing (paint/stain refresh)
- Cabinet refacing with new doors and veneer
- Hardware and hinge upgrades
- Custom color matching and consultation

Requirements vary by jurisdiction — always confirm with your city or county building department before starting work.

## DIY Cost: Redoing Kitchen Cabinets Yourself vs Hiring a Pro

Redoing kitchen cabinets yourself can save money on labor, but it takes real time and the right setup.

### DIY
- Fine for swapping hardware or hinges on a small number of doors
- Workable for a single-door touch-up or minor scuff repair
- Requires basic tools and a well-ventilated space

### Hire a pro
- Better for full-kitchen color changes where an uneven finish is hard to fix later
- Recommended when spray equipment and humidity-resistant coatings matter for durability
- Worth it for refacing, built-ins, or remodels involving multiple trades

## When to Call for Cabinet Refinishing & Remodeling

- Finish is peeling, chipping, or yellowing on multiple cabinets
- Cabinet color reads dated compared to the rest of the kitchen
- Drawers and doors stick or hinges no longer close properly
- Water damage or swelling has appeared near the sink or dishwasher
- Preparing a home for resale and want an updated kitchen without a full remodel budget
