Cabinet refinishing cleans, repairs, and refinishes existing cabinet boxes and doors in place of full replacement, using cabinet-grade primer and a sprayed or brushed topcoat for a smooth, durable result. Company Brand Name provides cabinet refinishing throughout Columbia for kitchens where the boxes and doors are structurally sound but the finish is dated, chipped, or discolored.

Because refinishing works with what's already installed, it's typically the most budget-friendly way to update a kitchen — and the most common starting point for homeowners comparing their options against full replacement.

## What's Included With Cabinet Refinishing

- **Typically included:** door and drawer removal, degreasing and cleaning, sanding, minor wood repair, priming, spray or brush finish coats, topcoat cure, hardware reinstall
- **Optional:** hardware and hinge upgrade, interior shelf touch-up, color change from the existing finish
- **Customer prep:** clearing cabinet interiors and countertops before the crew arrives

## How Our Cabinet Refinishing Process Works

- **Consultation** — In-home visit to assess cabinet condition and pick a finish color
- **Door removal** — Doors, drawers, and hardware are tagged and taken down
- **Clean and repair** — Surfaces are degreased and any chips or gouges filled
- **Sanding** — Doors and boxes are sanded to give the primer a surface to grip
- **Priming** — A cabinet-grade primer seals the wood or laminate
- **Finishing** — Paint or stain is applied with spray or brush equipment
- **Cure** — Finish sets under controlled conditions for a hard, durable coat
- **Final walkthrough** — Hardware is reinstalled and every door checked for fit

## Materials and Methods for Cabinet Refinishing

- **Sprayed cabinet-grade enamel** — A waterborne or hybrid enamel that levels smoother than brush paint, giving refinished cabinets a factory-style look that resists yellowing over time.
- **Conversion varnish topcoat** — A hard, moisture-resistant clear coat layered over the finish, chosen specifically to hold up through the Midlands' humid summers without softening or clouding.
- **Wood filler and epoxy repair** — Fills chipped edges, dents, and stripped screw holes before priming so the repair doesn't show once the new finish goes on.
- **Cabinet-grade primer/sealer** — A bonding primer built for previously painted, stained, or laminate surfaces, giving the topcoat something to hold onto instead of peeling within a season.
- **Soft-close hinges** — Frequently added during refinishing to eliminate slamming doors along with the finish refresh.

## How Long Does a Cabinet Refinishing Take?

Most refinishing jobs move quickly once prep begins, with the finish cure adding a day or two at the end.

- **Consult** — same day
- **Prep and repair** — 1–2 days
- **Finish coats** — 2–3 days
- **Cure and reinstall** — 1–2 days

## DIY vs Hiring a Pro for Cabinet Refinishing

### DIY
- Reasonable for a single small cabinet or accent piece
- Requires ventilation, careful masking, and patience between coats
- Brush marks and uneven sheen are common without spray equipment

### Hire a pro
- Spray equipment gives a smoother, more consistent finish across a full kitchen
- Faster turnaround than most DIY timelines
- Workmanship backed by a written estimate

## When to Call for Cabinet Refinishing

- Finish is chipping, peeling, or showing bare wood
- Cabinet color feels outdated next to newer countertops or flooring
- You want a budget-friendly alternative to full replacement
- Preparing the kitchen for resale
