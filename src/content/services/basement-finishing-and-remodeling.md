## How our Basement Finishing process works

- **Space Design Consultation** — The finisher and the homeowner stand in the unfinished basement and look at where the new wall will go.
- **Frame** — Studs go up for one partition. The rest of the basement stays unfinished.
- **Rough-in** — Cable is run along those studs before the drywall covers them.
- **Drywall** — Drywall is hung on that framed wall.
- **Flooring** — Vinyl plank goes down on the concrete. The sofa can stay in the room, covered.
- **Final touches** — The sofa is set back and the lamp is straightened. The room is already finished.

## Permits and codes for Basement Finishing

### Usually needs a permit
- Egress window installation
- New or modified electrical circuits
- Framing and wall layout changes
- Plumbing additions for a basement bathroom

### Usually no permit
- Cosmetic flooring installation
- Paint and trim work
- Furniture and fixture placement

Union County inspectors check this work at specific stages, confirm requirements with your local building office before you start.

## DIY vs hiring a pro for Basement Finishing

## Basement Finishing FAQs

What's the most expensive part of finishing a basement?
Usually electrical rough-in and egress window work, if a legal bedroom is the goal. Framing and drywall are comparatively cheap next to code-driven items.

Can I legally finish my own basement?
Homeowners can do the labor themselves in most New Jersey towns. The permit and inspection requirements still apply no matter who does the work.

Do basement finishing permits get pulled by homeowners or contractors?
Either can pull the permit, but whoever does the physical work is responsible for getting it inspected at each required stage.

What happens if I skip the permit on a finished basement?
It can come back to bite you at resale, during insurance claims, or if a future inspector finds unpermitted electrical or framing work. Not worth the risk on a space you plan to live in.

What does the finished basement look like?
Drywall, a flat ceiling, a floor, and ordinary furniture. The same window is still there. There is no post in the room.
