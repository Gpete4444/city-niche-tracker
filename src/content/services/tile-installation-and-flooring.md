## Materials & Methods for Tile Installation & Flooring

Porcelain tile tends to be more durable and less porous than ceramic, making it a strong choice for kitchens and bathrooms. Proper subfloor preparation and waterproofing (in wet areas) are essential for long-term durability — skipping these steps is a common cause of tile failure over time.

## How Long Does Tile Installation & Flooring Take?

Tile installation timelines depend on square footage and pattern complexity. A standard bathroom floor may take a few days, while larger kitchen areas or intricate patterns take longer. Waterproofing in wet areas requires cure time before tile can be installed.

## Tile Installation & Flooring FAQs

What's the difference between ceramic and porcelain tile?
Porcelain is denser and less porous, making it more durable and better suited to high-moisture areas like bathrooms.

How do I know if my subfloor needs repair?
Signs include soft spots, unevenness, or visible damage — we assess this during our initial consultation.

Can I install tile over existing flooring?
Sometimes, depending on the condition and type of the existing floor — we'll evaluate this during consultation.

## DIY vs. Professional Tile Installation & Flooring

Small, low-moisture areas may be reasonable for an experienced DIYer. Bathroom and shower tile installation is best left to a professional, since improper waterproofing or installation can lead to costly water damage.

## When to Call a Pro for Tile Installation & Flooring

Reach out if your tile is cracked or failing, flooring is outdated, or you're planning a kitchen or bathroom remodel that includes new flooring.

## Permits & Codes for Tile Installation & Flooring

Standard tile and flooring installation generally doesn't require a permit unless it's part of a larger remodel involving plumbing or structural changes. Verify specifics with local authorities as needed.
